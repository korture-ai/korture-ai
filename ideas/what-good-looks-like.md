# What good work with AI looks like, step by step

Korture Proof shows a finished piece of work as eight layers, from what you gave the AI to the finished piece. At each layer, the AI decides something on its own if you do not. This page says what that is, and what the people who build and study AI say good looks like there.

It is not a score. Use what fits how you want to work.

## 1. What you gave it

**What the AI decides if you do not:** it fills the gaps from what it can find, or it guesses.

- Give it what it cannot see for itself: decisions from meetings, steps that live in your head, the real file or screenshot instead of a description. ([Anthropic, Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#tell-claude-what-it-cant-see))
- Give the few things that matter, not everything you have. More material can make it lose focus. ([Anthropic, context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents))

## 2. Your ask

**What the AI decides if you do not:** how big the job is. It tends to build the smallest thing that fits.

- Say why, not only what. Would a colleague with no context understand your ask? ([Anthropic, prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices))
- Do your own thinking first. Write your own idea, even badly, before you ask. ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/is-chatgpt-really-rotting-our-brains), [Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/writes.html))

## 3. What finished looks like

**What the AI decides if you do not:** that done means "looks done". This is the layer where the AI always assumes something.

- Say what done looks like before it starts, as something that can pass or fail. ([Anthropic, Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#give-claude-a-check-to-run))
- Start from the person it is for. Write the result from their side first, then work back. ([Werner Vogels, Working Backwards](https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/2006/11/working_backwards.html))

## 4. The AI's first draft

**What the AI decides if you do not:** every choice you did not make: the approach, the tone, the length, the order.

- Ask for several options, then push on the one you like. ([Ethan Mollick](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/getting-started-with-ai-good-enough), [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Mar/11/using-llms-for-code/))
- Stay awake at the wheel. In one study, consultants using AI did better work on tasks inside its abilities, and worse on a task outside them. ([Ethan Mollick, the jagged frontier](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/centaurs-and-cyborgs-on-the-jagged))
- Before you accept a plan, imagine it already failed and list why. ([Gary Klein, the premortem](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem))

## 5. Your questions

**What the AI decides if you do not:** whether to ask you anything at all. Usually it fills the gap and carries on.

- When you are not sure what you want, ask the AI to interview you first. ([Anthropic, Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#brainstorm-with-claude-first))
- Ask it to argue against you: what am I missing, what would a critic say. ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/is-chatgpt-really-rotting-our-brains))

## 6. Your steers

**What the AI decides if you do not:** what your correction means.

- Steer early, and say what you want instead, not only "no". ([Ethan Mollick](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/getting-started-with-ai-good-enough))
- After two failed corrections on the same thing, start fresh with one better ask. ([Anthropic, Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#steer-the-session))

## 7. The AI's rework

**What the AI decides if you do not:** what "fixed" means.

- Check the rework against what you said done looks like, not against "find problems". ([Anthropic, Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices#review-against-your-spec))
- Keep doing the parts you want to stay good at. ([Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/does-ai-make-us-lazy/))

## 8. The finished work

**What the AI decides if you do not:** that it is finished.

- Only call it done when you have seen it work yourself. ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Dec/18/code-proven-to-work/))
- Before it leaves your hands, check the few things that must never be missed. ([Atul Gawande's checklist study](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2009/01/surgical-safety-checklist-drops-deaths-and-complications-by-more-than-one-third/))
- Explain it in your own words, without looking. Where you stall is what you do not know yet. ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/illusion-of-clarity))

## Why seeing your part matters

- People do not feel they own text the AI wrote, but they still put their name on it. More control over the AI raised the feeling of ownership. ([Draxler and others, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03283))
- Working with AI raised people's results, then lowered their motivation when they went back to working alone. ([Harvard Business Review, 2025](https://hbr.org/2025/05/research-gen-ai-makes-people-more-productive-and-less-motivated))
- People value what they made themselves far more than the same thing made for them, but only when it is finished. ([Norton, Mochon and Ariely, 2011](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-091.pdf))
